# FIREWATCH AI
## Understanding Global Fire Activity Through Satellite Data
**LH STUDIOS | NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026**

---

## 1. About the Project

FIREWATCH AI is a satellite-based fire activity analysis platform designed to help people explore, understand and compare historical burning activity across different regions of the world.

The platform addresses an important Earth observation challenge: MODIS and VIIRS satellite instruments detect thermal anomalies differently. Their spatial resolutions and detection characteristics differ, making their raw hotspot counts difficult to compare directly.

FIREWATCH AI brings these observations into a unified analytical environment, with a focus on scientifically documented MODIS–VIIRS harmonization.

Rather than simply displaying hotspots on a map, the platform aims to transform satellite observations into meaningful geographic, historical and statistical insights.

## 2. The Problem We Address

Wildfires and other sources of thermal activity affect ecosystems, communities, air quality, biodiversity and the environment.

Satellite instruments provide valuable observations of this activity. However, researchers and users face several challenges:

- Different satellite sensors produce observations with different characteristics.
- Raw MODIS and VIIRS hotspot counts are not directly equivalent.
- Historical fire activity can be difficult to investigate across long periods.
- Identifying unusual seasonal patterns requires historical comparisons.
- Large satellite datasets can be difficult to interpret without suitable visual tools.
- Users may struggle to understand what the data actually means.

These challenges make consistent, accessible historical analysis important.

## 3. Our Solution

FIREWATCH AI provides an integrated platform for geographic exploration, historical analysis and satellite-data comparison.

Users can select a geographic area, choose a date range and investigate available satellite observations through interactive maps, charts and analytical tools.

The platform focuses on:

1. Geographic selection and area-based analysis.
2. Historical MODIS and VIIRS observation exploration.
3. Spatial and temporal aggregation.
4. Sensor harmonization and comparison.
5. Monthly and yearly burning activity patterns.
6. Historical anomaly analysis.
7. Data exports and reproducible research.
8. AI-assisted explanations grounded in computed results.

## 4. Key Features

### Interactive Area of Interest Selection

Users can investigate a specific region using geographic selection tools, coordinates, radius-based selection or geographic presets.

This makes it possible to focus on a country, region or smaller study area rather than examining global data without context.

### Historical Fire Activity Analysis

The platform is designed to support historical exploration from 2000 to the present, subject to actual product availability and sensor coverage.

Users can investigate activity across different years, months and geographic regions.

### MODIS–VIIRS Harmonization

This is the central scientific component of FIREWATCH AI.

The system addresses differences between MODIS and VIIRS observations through documented spatial aggregation, temporal aggregation and calibration methods.

The objective is to create a more consistent analytical time series while preserving original sensor observations and communicating uncertainty.

### Burning Activity Calendar

A year-by-month calendar helps users identify seasonal patterns and periods of unusually high or low recorded activity.

Selecting a calendar cell can connect the historical result to its corresponding map view.

### Historical Baseline and Anomaly Analysis

Users can compare a selected period against historical values for comparable months.

This helps reveal unusual changes in recorded burning activity and provides context that a simple hotspot count cannot offer.

### Interactive Fire Map

The map provides geographic context for satellite observations and supports exploration of different sensors, historical periods and analytical layers.

### FIREWATCH AI

The integrated AI assistant helps users understand analytical results through natural-language questions.

It is designed to use actual application data and computed statistics rather than inventing measurements.

### Research Exports

The platform supports research-oriented outputs, including data and visualization exports, where implemented and verified.

These outputs can help users preserve results and conduct further analysis.

## 5. Why Is FIREWATCH AI Helpful?

FIREWATCH AI is useful because it connects satellite observations with the geographic and historical context needed to interpret them.

**For researchers:** It provides a structured environment for comparing satellite observations, exploring historical trends and examining harmonization methods.

**For environmental organizations:** It can help identify regions and periods that deserve further investigation.

**For students and educators:** It makes Earth observation concepts and satellite datasets easier to explore through interactive visualizations.

**For analysts:** It offers a way to investigate historical activity, compare regions and export analytical results.

**For the general public:** It can make complex satellite information more understandable and accessible.

The platform is intended to support research, exploration and informed environmental understanding. It does not replace official emergency services or operational wildfire monitoring systems.

## 6. Why MODIS–VIIRS Harmonization Matters

Combining observations from different satellite instruments is not as simple as adding their hotspot counts together.

MODIS and VIIRS differ in spatial resolution and detection characteristics. Direct comparisons can therefore create misleading conclusions about changes in burning activity.

FIREWATCH AI addresses this challenge by applying a documented analytical approach:

1. Collect original satellite observations.
2. Validate and preserve source information.
3. Apply appropriate confidence and source filters.
4. Aggregate observations onto a consistent analytical grid.
5. Calculate comparable temporal activity measures.
6. Estimate calibration relationships using overlapping observations.
7. Produce a harmonized analytical time series.
8. Evaluate agreement, uncertainty and limitations.

The scientific objective is comparability, not pretending that different sensors are identical.

## 8. Scientific Responsibility and Limitations

FIREWATCH AI recognizes that satellite hotspot observations have limitations.

Thermal anomalies do not necessarily represent confirmed wildfires. Satellite coverage, clouds, observation timing, detection thresholds and sensor characteristics can affect recorded activity.

Harmonization introduces methodological choices and uncertainty. Statistical anomalies do not automatically indicate danger or predict future events.

For these reasons, the platform distinguishes original observations, derived measurements, harmonized estimates and statistical interpretations.

Scientific transparency is a core part of the project.

## 9. Our Intended Impact

FIREWATCH AI aims to make historical satellite fire information easier to investigate and interpret.

By connecting geographic selection, satellite-data harmonization, historical analysis and AI-assisted explanations, the project seeks to reduce the gap between complex Earth observation datasets and understandable environmental insights.

Its broader goal is to support accessible, reproducible and scientifically responsible exploration of global burning activity.
